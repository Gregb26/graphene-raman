# Ab initio methods for optical conductivity / transport of graphene with vacancies, and ab initio T-matrix / Green's-function methods for point defects

Scope: (A) first-principles (or DFT-parametrized) calculations of σ(ω) or dc transport of defective graphene; (B) ab initio T-matrix / Green's-function / scattering-rate methods for point defects; (C) novelty check for the planned work (QE supercell M = M^L + M^NL, Wannier interpolation of M, exact local T-matrix on a 29-cell cluster, non-self-consistent Σ_k(ε) = c T̄_k(ε) following Kaasbjerg 2020, Wannier velocity with full Berry connection, Kubo–Greenwood bubble for σ(ω) of graphene with vacancies).

## (A) Which works compute the optical conductivity / absorption or dc transport of graphene with vacancies or point defects from first principles, and which cover finite ω vs only dc?

### Takeaway
No work was found that computes σ(ω) of *dilute, disorder-averaged* graphene vacancies from first-principles matrix elements. Finite-ω work on defective graphene falls into two families: (i) direct DFT supercell absorption spectra at very high, periodic defect concentrations (no disorder averaging, no scattering broadening), and (ii) pure tight-binding (TB) Kubo calculations in which a vacancy is simply a removed site (Yuan–Roldán–De Raedt–Katsnelson 2010/2011; Peres–Guinea–Castro Neto CPA models). The "ab initio-parametrized TB + real-space Kubo" family (Lherbier, Leconte, Charlier, Roche; Wehling/Katsnelson; Harju group; Ferreira & Mucciolo) is essentially dc only.

### Inferences
- The finite-ω literature on graphene with vacancies is TB-model-based: vacancy = deleted p_z site, or unitary scatterer in CPA/T-matrix. Its robust qualitative results (vacancy midgap peak, midgap→VHS transitions near ~t, IR background below 2μ from resonant scatterers; Yuan et al. 2011) are natural benchmarks that a DFT-based T-matrix σ(ω) should reproduce or correct.
- Direct DFT supercell spectra (Singh et al. 2013; Lee et al. 2020) sit at percent-level ordered concentrations and have no disorder-induced lifetime. They cannot give a Drude peak broadened by elastic scattering or the dilute-limit IR background, which is the regime the Σ = cT approach targets.
- The ab initio-parametrized TB + Kubo community (Charlier/Roche/Lherbier/Leconte, Katsnelson/Wehling, Harju) does include DFT information, but it passes through fitted short-range TB parameters and only computes dc quantities (mean free path, σ_sc, localization).
- The first-principles impurity-assisted absorption works (Kioupakis, Van de Walle) are a methodological precedent for "defect scattering + optical response from first principles". They use Coulomb-model charged impurities at second order (Born level) in bulk oxides/semiconductors, not neutral vacancies with a T-matrix.

### Gaps
- Radchenko/Shylau/Zozoulenko, Cresti et al., Trambly de Laissardière & Mayou, Garcia et al. (2015): only metadata was verified; whether any of them also shows finite-ω results was not checked (believed dc).
- The UCLouvain/ICN2 groups (Lherbier, Charlier, Roche, Cummings) may have finite-frequency Kubo–Bastin/KPM follow-ups for defective graphene; none was found in these searches.
- I did not find a DFT/RPA or GW-BSE study of *randomly placed dilute* vacancies in graphene with configuration averaging; all DFT optical work found uses ordered supercells.
- The exact disorder treatment behind Peres–Guinea–Castro Neto (2006) optical results (FSBA vs CPA vs self-consistent T-matrix) was not re-verified.

## (B) Ab initio T-matrix / Green's-function / scattering-rate methods for point defects: what each does (Born vs T-matrix, charged vs neutral, materials, graphene vacancies, optical?)

### Takeaway
Only two ab initio T-matrix lines exist in the literature found. The first is Kaasbjerg's (DTU) atomistic T-matrix: DFT/PAW-LCAO matrix elements in GPAW, non-self-consistent Σ = n_i T_kk, graphene vacancies included, dc Boltzmann transport, spectral functions and QPI, no optical conductivity. The second is its excitonic extension by Chan, Haber, Naik, Qiu, da Jornada (Nano Lett. 2026), which does compute optical absorption and PL, but for excitons in MoS₂ with S vacancies. The Bernardi (Caltech) electron–defect framework uses exactly the planned M^L + M^NL plane-wave/KB matrix element (QE, ΔV = V_KS(d) − V_KS(p), primitive-cell wavefunctions) and Wannier interpolation of M. It stays at Born level (Fermi golden rule), in bulk Si and Cu, for dc transport only. No KKR-Green-function impurity study of graphene transport or optics was found.

**B1 — Kaasbjerg atomistic T-matrix (DFT-based)**
- Kaasbjerg, "Atomistic T-matrix theory of disordered 2D materials: Bound states, spectral properties, quasiparticle scattering, and transport", Phys. Rev. B 101, 045433 (2020), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevB.101.045433, arXiv:1911.00530 (v1 1 Nov 2019): T-matrix formalism combined with "realistic density-functional theory (DFT) descriptions of the defects and their scattering matrix elements".   - Method details (ar5iv full text): implemented in **GPAW**, DFT-LDA, PAW, **DZP LCAO basis**, spin-orbit included. Matrix elements come from an LCAO expansion of pristine Bloch states, with defect potential = defective − pristine crystal potential and the PAW atomic corrections ΔV^a_{i1 i2} included, i.e. beyond the local potential and analogous to a nonlocal part. Supercell for the potential: 11×11 with 10 Å vacuum. T-matrix equation solved as a linear system (LAPACK). Self-energy is **non-self-consistent**, Σ^T_k(ε) = N_i T_kk(ε), lowest order in defect concentration. Transport is Boltzmann dc (σ = e² v_F² ρ τ/2 for graphene), with **no optical/finite-ω conductivity and no vertex corrections**. For graphene, resonant quasibound states "are inherent to the Dirac cone dispersion" and can be converged with dense k-sampling and few bands. The paper explicitly remarks that its fixed LCAO basis avoids "the additional step for the generation of the Wannier functions". — [ar5iv:1911.00530](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1911.00530)

### Inferences
- The planned matrix element (M^L via ΔṼ_L(q+G) with primitive-cell C_nk, plus a KB nonlocal term, from V_KS(d) − V_KS(p) in QE) is the Lu–Zhou–Bernardi (2019) construction. Wannier interpolation of M is the Lu–Park–Zhou–Bernardi (2020) idea. Neither is novel by itself; the novelty must come from combining them with the T-matrix, graphene vacancies, and optics.
- Kaasbjerg's T-matrix is solved in the Bloch-state (k, band) representation with PAW-LCAO matrix elements. The planned "exact local T-matrix on a 29-cell cluster" in a Wannier/real-space basis is a different route to the same Σ = c T_kk object. It would be a reasonable methodological variant (Kaasbjerg explicitly mentions and avoids Wannier functions); cross-checking against his graphene-vacancy results (11×11 supercell, non-SC Σ) is natural.
- The npj 2020 Wannier interpolation paper is most likely still Born level, since it computes relaxation times with the 2019 golden-rule framework and its abstract names no T-matrix. This is an inference; the full text was not checked for any higher-order treatment.
- The only first-principles T-matrix work that reaches an optical observable (Chan et al. 2026) does so for **excitons in a semiconductor**. There, the defect dresses a two-particle propagator, and no free-carrier Kubo bubble or intraband (Drude) channel is involved. Graphene's metallic/semimetallic Drude + interband σ(ω) with a disorder self-energy is a different, unaddressed problem.
- The Bena/Kumari 2026 preprint uses a *self-consistent* T-matrix (SCTMA) with DFT-derived TB. The non-self-consistent Σ = cT choice (following Kaasbjerg) should be justified against it, especially near the Dirac point where vacancy resonances make self-consistency matter.

### Gaps
- No verified KKR-Green-function study of vacancy/impurity scattering *in graphene* (Zeller/Dederichs/Mavropoulos/Ebert groups) was found. Absence is not proven; searches returned only generic KKR embedding and TB Green's-function works.
- Whether Lu et al. npj 2020 or any later Bernardi-group paper goes beyond Born was not verified in full text. The APS March 2021 abstract on charged defects (Lu et al.) was not opened.
- The "phonon–defect scattering: success and breakdown of the T-matrix approximation" work (APS March 2021 talk; a ResearchGate record exists) concerns phonons. Its authors and journal are UNVERIFIED and were not pursued.

## (C) Is there any published work computing σ(ω) of disordered graphene with a DFT-derived vacancy T-matrix plus a Wannier velocity with Berry connection? What is the closest existing work?

### Takeaway
None was found (literature checked through arXiv preprints of Jul–Sep 2026). Each ingredient exists separately, but no work combines ab initio vacancy T-matrix + disorder-averaged Σ + Kubo bubble for σ(ω) in graphene, let alone with a Wannier velocity including the Berry connection. The closest works are, by ingredient:
- Kaasbjerg PRB 2020: DFT T-matrix, graphene vacancies, dc only.
- Chan et al. Nano Lett. 2026: first-principles T-matrix + optical absorption, excitons in MoS₂.
- Yuan et al. PRB 2010/2011: σ(ω) of graphene with vacancies, pure TB.
- Lu/Bernardi 2019–2022: same M^L + M^NL and Wannier-interpolated M, Born, bulk Si/Cu, dc.
- Wehling et al. PRL 2010: DFT-parametrized resonant impurities + Kubo, dc.

### Inferences
- The combination "DFT-derived vacancy T-matrix → disorder-averaged Σ_k(ε) → Kubo–Greenwood σ(ω) (intraband Drude + interband) in graphene" appears to be **unpublished**. Also unpublished: an ab initio T-matrix built from QE-supercell M^L + M^NL with Wannier interpolation (instead of GPAW-LCAO), and a Wannier velocity with the full Berry-connection term validated against DFT momentum matrix elements inside a disordered-graphene Kubo bubble. Each of these is a defensible novelty claim.
- The safest framing is to present the work as extending Kaasbjerg's atomistic T-matrix (dc/spectral) to finite-frequency optics in graphene, with the Lu/Bernardi plane-wave + Wannier matrix-element machinery as the input. The closest optical analogue is Chan et al. 2026 (excitonic, MoS₂). TB results (Yuan et al. 2011: midgap→VHS peak, IR background) serve as qualitative benchmarks, and Wehling et al. 2010 / Lherbier et al. 2012 as the "DFT-parametrized TB" state of the art (dc only).
- Reviewers may raise two points that prior work treats differently. First, vertex corrections, which are absent in a bubble with only Σ, and which Kaasbjerg's Boltzmann τ_tr includes via (1 − cos θ) while a bare bubble does not. Second, self-consistency (SCTMA in Kumari/Bena 2026; CPA in Peres et al.). Both matter near the Dirac point for resonant vacancies.

- Wannier-interpolated optical conductivity infrastructure (Wang/Yates/Souza/Vanderbilt-type Berry-connection velocity; e.g. arXiv:2406.15466 "High-order and adaptive optical conductivity calculations using Wannier interpolation", authors UNVERIFIED) was not covered, as out of this sub-scope.
